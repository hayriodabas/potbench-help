How much mix does that pot take? Potbench measures the pot, takes away the root ball and the headspace, and tells you what to prepare, by the scoop.

Measure a pot

Pick the shape (straight, tapered, bowl or planter box), type the rim, the base and the inside height, or tap a common size. The pot is drawn in section as you type. Units follow your region and can be switched in Settings.

Root ball and headspace

Count the root ball as a share of the new pot, from the size of the old pot, or from its measured width and height. Headspace is the empty depth from the rim; a drainage layer and a settling allowance are optional.

Mixes

Build a mix by parts from the material library or add your own material. Ten starting mixes can be copied and changed. The three gauges (structure, holds water, drains) are a reference reading of the materials, not advice for a particular plant.

Scoops, bags and cost

Choose the scoop you hold and each material comes as a number of scoops. Set a bag size and price once per material and the repot shows bags to buy and the cost (Pro).

Pot it and the journal

Tick materials as they go in, correct what you really used, choose a follow-up week, and the plant is potted. Notes and photos follow in the journal; the Journal tab lists every entry across plants.

Collection batch (Pro)

Several planned repots that share a mix are added into one batch, with scoops and bags for all of them.

Backups

Settings › Export backup writes a file you keep; Restore from backup replaces what is on the device with it. Erase everything removes all data and photos.

Why a range, not one number?

Pots are rarely perfect shapes and root balls are approximate. The figure is the planning estimate; the band shows the spread to expect.

Does Potbench say which mix my plant needs?

No. It calculates amounts for the mix you choose. The starting mixes are common recipes to adjust, not a diagnosis or a promise about plant health.

I bought Pro on another device.

Open Settings › Restore purchases on the new device, signed in to the same Apple Account.

How do I change the language?

Settings › App language opens the iOS per-app language setting.
