Support

Potbench measures the pot, takes away the root ball and the headspace, shows how the amount was worked out, and counts the scoops with you as you pour.

Measure the pot

Pick the shape (straight, tapered, bowl or planter box) and type the rim, the base and the inside height, or pick a common size. US nursery sizes (#1, #2, #3, #5) and European C-containers are there too; brands differ, so check the base of the pot. If you filled the pot with water and measured it, type that volume: it wins over the measurements. Units follow your region and can be switched in Settings.

Root ball and the step up

When a plant moves up a size, choose Moving up and the old pot: its volume counts as the root ball, less what you loosen away. Potbench also compares the two pots; guides suggest a pot 1 to 2 inches (2.5 to 5 cm) wider, and a note says when the new pot is much bigger. For a cutting, use a share of the pot; for an odd root ball, type its width and height.

Headspace and settling

Headspace is the empty depth below the rim, about 1 inch (2.5 cm), half that in small pots. Fresh mix sinks on the first watering, about 15 % for bark and coir mixes and 30 to 40 % with garden soil, so that much extra is prepared. The lines under the amount show each step, and they add up to the number shown.

Why there is no drainage layer

University extension guides advise against a layer of gravel or shards at the bottom of a pot: it does not help water drain and keeps the soil above it wetter. Potbench fills the pot with one mix from top to bottom. A bottom filler is offered only for very large planters, to save mix and weight.

Mixes

Build a mix by parts from the material library, add your own material, or copy one of ten starting recipes. The mix is drawn as a measuring jug, one layer per material. The three scales (structure, holds water, drains) describe the mix; they are not advice for a particular plant.

Measure out and pot

Choose the scoop you hold (cup, quart, half a litre and so on). Every part becomes a number of scoops, rounded to the half scoop so the scoops add up. On the measuring screen each tap on a row, or on the large key, adds a scoop; the jug fills to its line and the next material is marked. Undo takes the last scoop back. The screen stays awake while it is open. Potting keeps what really went in.

Bags and cost (Pro)

Set the bag a material comes in, as printed, and its price. US potting-mix bags are sold in dry quarts (1 qt is 1.1 L) and cubic feet; Potbench reads them that way. The plan shows how many bags to buy, what is left in the last one and the cost.

Batch plants (Pro)

Several planned repots that share a mix become one batch: one jug, one set of scoops and bags, measured out in one go. Each plant's journal keeps its share.

Journal and backups

Every repot keeps its pot, its mix and the amounts, with dated notes, photos and an optional check-back reminder. Settings › Export backup writes a file you keep; Restore from backup replaces what is on the device with it. Erase everything removes all data and photos.

Why a range, not one number?

Pots are rarely perfect shapes and root balls are approximate. The amount is a planning estimate; the band shows the spread to expect.

Does Potbench say which mix my plant needs?

No. It works out amounts for the mix you choose. The starting recipes are common mixes to adjust, not a diagnosis or a promise about plant health.

The scoops add up to a little more than the amount.

Each material is rounded to the half scoop so that you can measure it by hand; the plan says when the rounded total differs.
